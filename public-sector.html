Executive Summary

A U.S. public-sector agency required high-volume invoice processing with accurate coding, documented approvals, reconciliations, and audit-ready support. Close timing hinged on bank and subledger reconciliations, accruals, and fast issue resolution, while working-capital visibility needed aging analysis across departments.

I supported high-volume AP and month-end close while improving workflow clarity, exception handling, and working-capital visibility without weakening documentation controls. In the public sector, a faster process that weakens documentation is a failure — every gain here was designed to survive an auditor’s review.

01Business problem

Public-sector finance operates under formal documentation, approval, reconciliation, and audit requirements. Speed matters, but not at the expense of the approval chain, the reconciliation evidence, or the audit trail that a public agency is accountable for.

02My role

AP workflow — Owned. Reconciliations & close schedules — Owned. AR aging analysis — Owned. Final approvals — Management-owned.

03Approach & controls

High invoice volume created constant pressure to move faster, while month-end close depended on reconciliations, accruals, prepaids, and reclasses landing on schedule. Working-capital visibility required disciplined AR aging analysis across departments. The challenge was increasing throughput without weakening approval evidence, reconciliation discipline, or auditability.

  • Managed a monthly AP process handling 1,000+ invoices, standardizing approval workflows and improving processing efficiency by 35%.
  • Performed system-to-system reconciliations across bank, subledger and GL, plus journal entries and close schedules, supporting a 3-day reduction in close-cycle timelines.
  • Analyzed AR aging and coordinated cross-functional resolution, accelerating collections by 18%.
  • Balanced process efficiency against public-sector documentation and control requirements at every step.

Improvements were designed control-first: a faster workflow only shipped if it preserved evidence and approvals. Process improvements were accepted only when approval evidence, reconciliation support, and auditability were preserved.

06What I'd improve next

  • In controlled environments, documentation and approval evidence are part of the deliverable — not administrative afterthoughts.
  • AR aging is both a financial-visibility and operating follow-up tool; prioritized follow-up changes outcomes.
  • Recommendation: automate matching on high-volume PO-backed categories to cut manual coding further while preserving the documented-approval trail.
