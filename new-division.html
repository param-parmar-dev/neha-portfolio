Building FP&A Infrastructure for a New Consulting Division

Building the planning, revenue-recognition, and forecasting infrastructure for a brand-new business line — from zero history to a decision-ready, driver-based operating model.

Executive Summary

A new consulting division launched with no established FP&A infrastructure, no reliable history, and no mature budget baseline, inside a multi-business-unit professional-services company (15+ departments). Multiple engagement models meant pipeline, cash, billings, recognized revenue, capacity, vendors, and headcount all behaved differently — and contract credits could overstate genuinely new revenue if taken at headline value.

I built the operating model and forecasting infrastructure from scratch, and co-developed revenue schedules aligned with company accounting policy, designing the reporting to keep growth honest by separating new cash from reallocated credits. The result was a multi-year, three-scenario model leadership could use for planning, capacity, and resource decisions.

01Business problem

Standing up finance for a new business line is a different discipline from running an established one. There is no run-rate to lean on, no seasoned baseline, and every assumption has to be built, documented, and defended — while the business is already moving.

02My role

Driver-based model — Owned. Forecast assumptions — Owned. Pipeline finance requirements — Owned. Pipeline implementation — Partnered. Revenue schedules — Built with Accounting. Accounting policy/sign-off — Accounting-owned.

03Approach & controls

Several engagement models made the economics genuinely complex: bookings, billings, cash, deferred revenue, and recognized revenue all moved on different clocks. The reporting-integrity risk was specific — contract credits, if counted at headline value, would make reallocated revenue look like new growth.

  • Built a multi-year monthly P&L with base, upside, and downside scenarios — documented assumptions, headcount, COGS, and OpEx.
  • Built the finance requirements for a live pipeline and co-developed revenue schedules tied to signed contracts and budgets, aligned with Accounting's revenue-recognition policy.
  • Designed a usage-based prepaid-subscription tracker and a handoff guide so business and AP users could run monthly accounting.
  • Benchmarked comparable launches and analyzed contract economics to separate new cash from reallocated credits.

The model ran driver-first from opportunities, conversion assumptions and capacity into forecast revenue, gross margin and operating income; accounting recognition was kept in a separate contract-based revenue schedule aligned with ASC 606. Each scenario was tied to explicit operating assumptions and management actions so the model showed both what could happen and what would have to change to produce each outcome.

06What I'd improve next

  • The first thing a new business line needs from finance is not a budget — it is a defensible set of documented assumptions everyone can argue with.
  • Reporting integrity is a design decision made early; retrofitting honesty into a growth narrative is far harder.
  • Recommendation: integrate signed-contract data with revenue schedules while keeping pipeline-linked forecasting separate from accounting recognition; reconcile bookings, billings, deferred revenue and recognized revenue monthly.
