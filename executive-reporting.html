Executive Reporting & Multi-Entity Consolidation Controls

Owning accuracy review and executive commentary across a multi-entity reporting cycle — and building controls that catch decision-distorting errors before delivery.

Executive Summary

A multi-business-unit professional-services company (15+ departments) required consolidated quarterly reporting across multiple legal entities, including P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, MD&A, and product-revenue commentary.

I owned the accuracy review and executive narrative, and established a two-level quality-control standard: mathematical accuracy and narrative accuracy, gated separately, validated for executive review. The reporting cycle's defining control outcome was intercepting sign-logic and unexplained-movement issues before they could distort executive commentary.

01Business problem

The organization operated several business units across distinct legal entities, each contributing to a consolidated executive package presented quarterly to senior leadership. Reporting fed real capital and operating decisions, so the credibility of every figure — and every sentence of commentary — carried weight.

02My role

Accuracy review — Owned. Executive commentary — Owned. Control design — Owned. AI-assisted review workflows — Partnered. Final organizational sign-off — Leadership/Controller.

03Approach & controls

Consolidated packages are where small errors become expensive. Arithmetic can foot perfectly while formatting, sign logic, source hierarchy, or narrative still communicates the wrong conclusion. A favorable/unfavorable flag reversed, or an unexplained balance-sheet movement absorbed into the story to hold a deadline, can mislead decision-makers even when the underlying arithmetic appears correct.

The core risk: a package that looks finished is not the same as a package that is correct. Distinguishing the two, under close-cycle time pressure, was the problem to solve.
  • Owned review of variance dollars, percentages, favorable/unfavorable logic, EBITDA margin, and cross-statement consistency across entities.
  • Authored executive commentary connecting results to drivers, timing, risks, and recommended actions.
  • Escalated unexplained balance-sheet and cash-flow movements for investigation rather than smoothing them into the narrative.
  • Partnered on AI-assisted P&L, MD&A, and balance-sheet review workflows — each operating strictly behind a human reconciliation gate.

Every package moved through a structured gate: reconcile the math, then interrogate the narrative against the drivers, then separate critical issues from cosmetic ones on an exception log. The decision rule was explicit — if a movement cannot be explained from the data, it is escalated, not narrated. That rule prevented unexplained movements from being normalized into commentary simply to meet a deadline.

The validated finance layer is the input to enterprise distribution, not a parallel copy of it. Reviewed outputs from this workflow feed the BI team's enterprise dashboards on a scheduled refresh, so the numbers leadership sees company-wide are the same ones finance signed off on.

06What I'd improve next

  • The highest-value control in executive reporting is not a faster close — it is a reviewer empowered to escalate an anomaly instead of absorbing it.
  • AI belongs in the mechanical tie-out, never in the sign-off; a model cannot verify what it cannot reconcile.
  • Recommendation: push routine tie-out into automated pre-checks so scarce human review time concentrates on narrative judgment and escalation — the part only a person should own.
