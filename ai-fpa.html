AI-Assisted FP&A Workflows & Governance

Using AI to build repeatable, auditable finance workflows — while keeping financial logic, validation, and final judgment firmly under human control.

Finance logic & controls — OwnedWorkflow requirements — OwnedPrompt/workflow design — OwnedCode implementation — AI-assistedValidation — OwnedFinal approval — Human-owned
Executive Summary

Recurring variance analysis, MD&A, balance-sheet review, BvA delivery, and data prep across a multi-business-unit professional-services company (15+ departments) needed consistent logic across many cycles. AI output could not be trusted without readiness checks, reconciliation to the system of record, review gates, and clear attribution — and reusable workflows needed documentation so they supported the whole team.

I built a library of governed AI-assisted finance workflows and specified the human controls around them. The differentiator is not that AI was used — it is the governance: a reconciliation gate on every output, explicit attribution, and human approval. AI output was never treated as verification: every financial output remained source-reconciled and human-approved.

01Business problem

The finance function ran the same analytical patterns every cycle. AI offered obvious leverage — but in finance, unverified speed is a liability. The task was to capture the leverage without ever compromising accuracy, confidentiality, or accountability.

02My role

Finance logic & controls — Owned. Workflow requirements — Owned. Prompt/workflow design — Owned. Code implementation — AI-assisted. Validation — Owned. Final approval — Human-owned.

03Approach & controls

An LLM pointed at a spreadsheet will produce confident output whether or not it is correct. Without readiness checks, source reconciliation, review gates, and clear attribution, that output is a material finance-control risk. Reusable workflows also risked becoming one analyst’s undocumented black box.

  • Owned the month-over-month P&L variance-analysis workflow and a source-ingestion / prompt-reader workflow.
  • Partnered on BvA, department-BvA, MD&A, balance-sheet analysis, and source-ingestion templates and reusable finance context files.
  • Owned business requirements for scheduled data-sync pipelines and vendor-spend scripts, then used AI-assisted implementation to build them from documented finance requirements, then validated outputs against source data.
  • Specified the human controls: source readiness, reconciliation thresholds, a reproduction plan, explicit approval, blocker handling, and required human sign-off.

Every workflow ran the same path: input → AI-assisted analysis → reconciliation gate → human approval → validated output. An attribution matrix recorded exactly what was Owned, Built, Partnered, or AI-assisted from documented requirements. AI output cannot serve as financial verification; reconciliation and human approval remain required before release.

06What I'd improve next

  • The value of AI in finance is in the governance around it, not the model itself.
  • Documented attribution is what makes AI-assisted work auditable — and therefore trustworthy to a controller.
  • Recommendation: add automated provenance tagging to every AI-assisted figure so source-to-output lineage is visible on the face of the deliverable, making the reconciliation gate self-documenting.
