Departmental Budget vs. Actual (BvA) & Business Partnering

Turning a recurring five-department BvA cycle into a controlled management workflow — with a reconciled FP&A baseline and collaborative department review.

Monthly BvA cycle — OwnedReconciliation — OwnedVariance analysis/commentary — OwnedDepartment review — Partnered
Executive Summary

Five departments within a multi-business-unit professional-services company (15+ departments overall) required monthly account-level variance analysis, concise executive commentary, and leadership-ready review. Source files carried real-world hazards — budget-version differences, pre-close actuals, missing departments, misclassified segments, and mapping issues.

I ran the monthly cycle end to end and established a reconciled FP&A baseline for departmental review and variance commentary while keeping department leaders as partners. The defining outcome was tracing a product-label defect that caused a roughly $50K reporting omission across a transaction file exceeding 100,000 rows.

01Business problem

Departmental leaders and executives relied on the monthly BvA to understand where the business stood against plan. That made the report both a management tool and a point of potential friction — its numbers had to be authoritative and its commentary had to be useful to the people being measured.

02My role

Monthly BvA cycle — Owned. Reconciliation — Owned. Variance analysis/commentary — Owned. Department review — Partnered.

03Approach & controls

Each input introduced a different control risk: department completeness, budget-version accuracy, close readiness, segment classification, or mapping integrity. Newly launched departments compounded the problem: with no baseline, standard variance percentages were meaningless and needed a different analytical frame.

  • Ran monthly cycles, resolved department scope from live management data, and reconciled large stacked transaction files to department reports.
  • Added business context the ledger could not supply — commissions, travel, transfers, headcount, new revenue streams.
  • Traced a budgeted-expense discrepancy to omitted departments and upstream segment classification rather than forcing a premature fix.
  • Established a reconciled FP&A baseline while keeping department leaders involved in validating operating drivers and commentary.

The workflow was fixed: readiness check, reconciliation, analysis, reviewer gate, department review, delivery. Commentary followed a consistent anatomy — Result · Driver · Timing · Risk · Action — so leaders received judgment, not just numbers. Core control: unresolved variances or mapping issues were traced to source before commentary was finalized.

06What I'd improve next

  • A report that looks right and a report that is right are different artifacts; the discipline of tracing to source is what separates them.
  • A controlled baseline and collaborative business partnering are compatible — the numbers can be firm while the relationship stays partnered.
  • Recommendation: convert the readiness check into an automated pre-flight validating budget version, department completeness, and segment mapping at intake — catching the omitted-department error class before analysis begins.
