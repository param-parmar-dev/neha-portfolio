Enterprise KPI Dashboards & Reporting Controls

Designing and safeguarding recurring KPI dashboards leadership uses to monitor and steer performance — and tracing hidden data-integrity defects before they distort reported results.

Finance logic — OwnedDiagnostics — OwnedValidation — OwnedDashboard design — BuiltDashboard delivery — Partnered
Executive Summary

Executive decisions across a multi-business-unit professional-services company (15+ departments) depended on recurring dashboards spanning daily, weekly, monthly, and six-month cadences. Their formulas, date boundaries, lookup ranges, and source tabs could silently distort results — a small formula error is a real gap in reported performance.

I owned the finance logic, diagnostics, validation, and delivery of the dashboard suite, and instituted a data-integrity discipline: every fix was generalized across periods, tested for overlap and double-counting, and regression-tested before rollout. The signature outcome was tracing a hidden period-boundary defect that was excluding final-day sales.

01Business problem

Leadership steered the business off a set of live performance dashboards. Because those dashboards were consulted constantly and rebuilt rarely, any defect embedded in a formula or date boundary would propagate quietly across every period without becoming obvious in the reported output.

02My role

Finance logic — Owned. Diagnostics — Owned. Validation — Owned. Dashboard design — Built. Dashboard delivery — Partnered.

03Approach & controls

Reporting logic is deceptively fragile. A timestamp boundary that excludes the final day of a period, a lookup range that drifts, or a hardcoded date buried in a scorecard can each misstate performance while looking entirely plausible. Fixes carried their own risk: a correction applied carelessly across many periods can introduce double-counting.

  • Diagnosed a hidden timestamp boundary that excluded every final-day sale from period totals, and validated the corrected date-boundary logic across historical periods and tested for overlap/double-counting before rollout.
  • Reverse-engineered and rebuilt a full-year weekly tracker, correcting lookup, range, and hardcoded-date defects.
  • Rebuilt hardcoded scorecards into live formulas and designed self-contained dashboards with fallback data-upload paths.
  • Built and partnered on the broader dashboard suite across requirements, finance logic, validation, and delivery.

Each defect was worked from symptom to root cause, corrected generically, then regression-tested against known-good periods before release. A reporting fix was considered complete only after it reproduced correctly across historical comparison periods without introducing overlap or downstream defects.

06What I'd improve next

  • The most dangerous reporting errors are the ones that look right; only regression testing reliably surfaces them.
  • Self-contained dashboards with explicit source hierarchy age far better than clever interconnected ones.
  • Recommendation: wrap the reusable formula patterns in an automated regression suite that runs on every refresh, so a boundary or lookup defect is flagged at the moment it appears.
